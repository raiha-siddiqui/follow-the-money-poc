Follow the Money

An agent that follows ransomware and scam payments hop by hop across a blockchain, under a fixed budget — then a red-team critic challenges every claim and a human approves the report. Watch it trace below.

Research prototype. It produces leads for human investigators, not findings of ownership or guilt. Nothing is ever sent to an exchange or authority. The demo graph below is fictional.

    4 · The tracing ideas inside

    Common-input clustering

    Coins spent together in one transaction usually share an owner, so their addresses form a wallet. Very large clusters are treated as services and become stopping points, not false links.

    Change detection

    Senders get leftovers back as “change”. Signals: paying back to one of the sender’s own addresses (strongest), same address type, a non-round amount. Treated as a probability, never a fact.

    Peel chains

    A long chain that peels off a small payment at each hop and sends the rest onward. Common when cashing out stolen funds — and easy to follow once recognised.

    CoinJoin & TRON

    CoinJoin deliberately mixes many users, so clustering is switched off there. On TRON (USDT) there is no change at all: the agent follows token transfer events and watches Tether’s freeze and unfreeze events.

    5 · Checked against a real court case

    A public US Department of Justice filing about the 2021 Colonial Pipeline ransom describes this hop. Replaying recorded blockchain data, the tracer reproduced it exactly.
